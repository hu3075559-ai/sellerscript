TikTok Shop Banned Words List for Product Listings: What to Avoid in 2026

First, the honest answer: TikTok Shop publishes no official banned-words list. There is no PDF you can download and memorise. Listings and videos are judged against policy pages — the Product Listing Policy, the Content Policy, and the "How to Avoid Misleading Content" guidance — which prohibit misleading claims, exaggerated promises, false comparisons and false authority claims. So the list below is the practical one: the 9 risk categories and 26 word patterns that SellerScript's banned-words checker scans for, built from those public policy patterns, each with a safer rewrite. Use it as a first pass before you publish — not as a guarantee.

The list: 9 categories, 26 patterns

Every entry below is a pattern the banned-words checker flags, with the reason and the safer way to say it. The list is SellerScript's own guidance built from public marketplace advertising-policy patterns. It is not an official TikTok list, and passing it does not guarantee approval.

1 · Medical claims — the highest-risk category

"cure", "heal", "treat (acne / eczema / pain / disease)"Non-drug products cannot claim to cure, heal or treat conditions. TikTok Shop's own guidance lists exaggerated medicinal claims for non-medicinal products among the prohibited exaggerated promises. Safer: talk about routine and feel, not treatment.
"lose 10 lbs", "lose weight", "burn fat", "slimming"Specific weight-loss promises are prohibited for most product types, and weight-loss effect claims are restricted even without a number attached. Safer: share your routine and habits, not a promised body change.
"detox""Detox" implies the product cleanses the body — a medical effect ordinary products cannot claim. Safer: describe the ingredients and how the product fits your routine.
"erase wrinkles", "wrinkle-free skin", "anti-aging guarantee"Reversal claims for cosmetics are treated as drug claims. Safer: use appearance language — "skin looks smoother".
"antibacterial", "kills 99.9% (bacteria / germs)"Antimicrobial kill claims need registered disinfectant status and lab proof in most markets. Safer: "helps keep surfaces clean", and point to the ingredient list.
"no side effects", "safe for everyone"Safety absolutes for anything applied to the body are high-risk. Safer: list ingredients and materials, and say who the product is designed for.

2 · Unverifiable promises

"guaranteed", "100% …", "permanent", "forever"Outcome guarantees and permanence claims are treated as deceptive unless they state a checkable fact (for example, a fibre content taken from the label). Safer: describe what the product does, and state a tested duration — "lasted through 30 washes".
"instant results", "overnight results", "miracle"Instant-result promises are a top rejection reason in ad review, and TikTok Shop's guidance names miracle effects and instant effects among the prohibited exaggerated promises. Safer: give a realistic timeframe from your own use.

3 · Superlatives

"best", "cheapest", "#1", "number one", "perfect", "flawless"Unranked superlatives need proof you likely cannot attach, and perfection claims overstate results and trigger manual review. Safer: use a concrete differentiator — size, material, speed, bundle.

4 · Fake urgency and scarcity

"only today", "last chance", "ends tonight", "act now", "limited time"If the offer does not really end, countdown pressure counts as deceptive. Safer: use the real promotion end date, or drop the urgency.
"selling out fast", "almost gone", "limited stock", "only 3 left"Scarcity claims must match real inventory; an exact stock-count claim must match real, current stock. Safer: show the real stock level from your listing, or drop the count.

5 · False authority and proof claims

"clinically proven", "doctor recommended", "dermatologist approved"Only usable with real studies or endorsements you can produce on request. Safer: name the actual test or ingredient standard instead.
"FDA approved", "approved by …", "official"Claiming FDA approval for a product the FDA has not approved is a top takedown reason in beauty, supplements and health gadgets; implying platform or regulator endorsement you do not have is deceptive. Safer: drop the claim, or name the real certification with its scope.

6 · Misleading pricing

"free" (on a paid product)"Free" on a paid product reads as a pricing trick and draws complaints — the checker exempts "free shipping". Safer: say exactly what is included — "bonus X included".
"discount up to 50%""Up to" discounts must apply to a real share of the range. TikTok Shop's guidance also requires prices and promotions in content to match the product detail page, with conditions stated explicitly. Safer: state the exact discount on this product.

7 · Income claims, clickbait and sensitive comparisons

"get rich", "make money fast", "passive income guarantee"Income promises are banned in marketplace ads, full stop. Safer: sell the tool or the process, never the income.
"you won't believe", "shocking", "secret hack brands don't want you to know"Clickbait phrasing lowers trust scores and can limit reach. Safer: lead with the actual result or demo.
"before and after"Before/after presentations are restricted for beauty, weight and health products, and TikTok Shop's guidance lists before-and-after images for acne, whitening and anti-hair-loss products among the prohibited comparisons. Safer: show your own routine in progress instead of a comparison — and remember a disclaimer is allowed only for before-and-after results and time-based results.

Where the words hide

Review does not stop at the title. TikTok Shop's Content Policy applies to every element of promoted content — titles, descriptions, video, images and LIVEs — and the Product Listing Policy prohibits irrelevant or deceptive descriptions, brands or attributes in titles. Check all four places:

Title. Superlatives and "free" sneak in here most often. Build the title with the title optimizer from your core keyword, then paste it through the checker before publishing.

Description. Medical claims and "100%" promises hide in bullet points. Rewrite each bullet as a checkable fact: material, size, what's included, how it is used.

Video script and captions. Urgency ("only today") and income promises usually appear in the spoken script, not the listing. Write the script with the script generator, then paste the finished script — captions and hashtags included — into the checker.

Hashtags. A tag can carry a claim too. Keep tags descriptive of the product; the hashtag generator builds them from your category and product keyword, and the guide to best hashtags for TikTok Shop products explains which ones earn a slot.

What a clean scan does not mean

A clean scan does not mean the listing is compliant, and it does not mean the listing will be approved. Three gaps to keep in mind: category rules — beauty, supplements, food and health gadgets carry extra restrictions that a general word list cannot cover; images and video — a claim made visually (a before/after photo, a filter that changes the product) is judged even when the words are clean; and policy drift — TikTok Shop updates its policy pages, so a word that passed last month may not pass this month. When in doubt, the policy centre for your market is the authority, not this guide and not the checker.

Before you publish: a 2-minute checklist

1. Title, description and script all pasted through the banned-words checker; every flag rewritten, not just deleted.

2. No medical, weight-loss or "detox" claim for an ordinary product; no "cure", "heal" or "treat" language.

3. No superlative without proof you can attach; no "guaranteed", "100%", "instant" or "permanent" promise.

4. No urgency or scarcity unless it matches the real promotion end date and real stock.

5. No authority claim (FDA, clinically proven, doctor recommended, official) you cannot produce evidence for.

6. Prices and attributes in the video match the product detail page exactly.

FAQ

Is there an official TikTok Shop banned words list?

No. TikTok Shop does not publish a single banned-words list. Listings and content are judged against policy pages such as the Product Listing Policy, the Content Policy, and the "How to Avoid Misleading Content" guidance, which prohibit misleading claims, exaggerated promises, false comparisons and false authority claims. Any "banned words list" you find — including the one in this guide — is a practical summary built from those policy patterns, not an official document.

Which words are most likely to get a TikTok Shop listing flagged?

The repeat offenders are medical and treatment claims for ordinary products (cure, heal, lose 10 lbs, detox), unverifiable superlatives (best, cheapest, #1, perfect), fake urgency (only today, selling out fast, only 3 left), income promises (get rich, make money fast), and false authority claims (clinically proven, FDA approved, doctor recommended). TikTok Shop's own guidance lists exaggerated medicinal claims, miracle effects and instant results among the prohibited exaggerated promises.

Does passing a banned-words checker mean my listing is compliant?

No. A clean scan is a first pass only. TikTok Shop reviews images, video, pricing and the product detail page as well as words, and policies differ by country and product category and change over time. The checker on this site runs entirely in your browser against its own rule library and cannot guarantee approval — always verify against the current TikTok Shop policy centre for your market and category.

What happens if a TikTok Shop listing uses banned words?

According to TikTok Shop's policy pages, violations can lead to content removal, violation points, feature restrictions or account restrictions, and severe violations such as promoting a harmful product can lead to account suspension. The "How to Avoid Misleading Content" guidance states the same range: content removal or account suspension depending on severity.
