15-Second vs 30-Second TikTok Sales Script Structure: Which Length Fits Your Product?

Short answer: a 15-second script is a fast pitch — hook, one demo line, offer. A 30-second script is the full pitch — hook, pain, demo, proof, offer. They are not the same script at different speeds; they are two different structures with two different word budgets. At a natural speaking pace of about 150 words per minute, 15 seconds holds roughly 35–45 words and 30 seconds holds roughly 75–90 words. Pick the length by what the buyer still needs to hear — not by how much you want to say.

Side by side: the two structures

Same four-beat skeleton (hook → pain → demo → offer), different amounts of room. The 15-second version compresses it; the 30-second version completes it.

15 seconds — the fast pitch (35–45 words)

Beat 1 · Hook, 0–3 seconds (~8 words)One line that names the buyer's problem or the result. No setup, no greeting, no brand name yet.
Beat 2 · Demo, ~4–10 seconds (~15 words)One thing the product does, shown on camera while you say it. One feature, one shot. This is the whole persuasion — there is no second demo.
Beat 3 · Offer, ~11–15 seconds (~12 words)Price and where to tap. The call to action points at the product link; the price in the video must match the listing exactly, per TikTok Shop's misleading-content guidance.

What is missing, on purpose: the pain point and the proof line. A 15-second viewer has already felt the problem (that is why they stopped scrolling) — you do not need to explain it, and you have no time to prove anything beyond the demo itself.

30 seconds — the full pitch (75–90 words)

Beat 1 · Hook, 0–3 seconds (~8 words)Same opening as the 15-second version. The hook does not get longer just because the video does.
Beat 2 · Pain, ~4–10 seconds (~18 words)Name the frustration in the buyer's own words, and why the obvious alternatives fail. This is what the extra time is mostly for — a buyer who recognises their own problem keeps watching.
Beat 3 · Demo, ~11–20 seconds (~25 words)The product solving the problem on camera, with one or two features shown — not listed. Show the size, the fit, the result; say only what the camera cannot say.
Beat 4 · Proof, ~21–25 seconds (~12 words)One checkable fact that answers the buyer's likeliest doubt: how long one fill lasts, what is included, that it runs quietly. A fact from the listing or the packaging — never a superlative ("best", "#1") or a claim you cannot back up.
Beat 5 · Offer, ~26–30 seconds (~15 words)Price, what is included, and the tap. Same rule as the short version: the offer in the video must match the product detail page.

Worked example: the same product, both lengths

Product: a foldable mini humidifier, $12.99, audience "students in small dorm rooms". The two scripts below are written to the budgets above and counted word by word.

15-second script — 35 words

"Stop waking up with a dry throat. This foldable humidifier fits any dorm desk, runs all night on one fill, and costs less than two takeout coffees. Tap the link — it is $12.99 today."

35 words, about 14 seconds at 150 words per minute. Hook (dry throat) → demo (fits any desk, runs all night) → offer ($12.99, tap the link). No pain beat, no proof beat — the demo carries both.

30-second script — 80 words

"Stop waking up with a dry throat. Dorm rooms dry out fast, and most humidifiers are too bulky for a small desk. This one folds flat, fits in a drawer, and runs all night on a single fill. Fill it at night, wake up to a room that feels less dry. No filter to replace, no noise while you sleep. It is $12.99, and the link below takes you straight to the listing. Tap it before your next dry morning."

80 words, about 32 seconds at 150 words per minute — trim two or three words before filming to land under 30. The extra beats are the pain (bulky humidifiers, small desks) and the proof (no filter, no noise). Notice the 30-second script is not the 15-second one with extra adjectives: it adds whole beats.

When to choose which length

Choose 15 seconds when the product is simple, inexpensive and self-explanatory — the viewer can judge it from a single demo shot. A phone stand, a cable organiser, a foldable bottle: if one shot answers the only question a buyer has, the short version respects their time and the offer arrives before they scroll.

Choose 30 seconds when the buyer needs a reason first. Three signs: the problem is one they have not named yet (they live with it, they do not search for it); a feature needs showing, not just naming (how it folds, how it fits); or a doubt needs one proof line (is it noisy? does it need a filter? what is included?). If you cannot name what the extra 15 seconds are for, make the 15-second version — a padded short script is worse than a short script.

When in doubt, film the 15-second version first. It is faster to shoot, faster to re-shoot, and it forces the discipline the long version needs: one hook, one demo, one offer. If the comments ask a question the short video could not answer, that question is the pain or proof beat of your 30-second version.

Three mistakes that break both formats

1. Cramming the 30-second script into 15 seconds. Reading twice as fast sounds rushed and the demo gets squeezed out. Write a separate shorter script; do not speed-read the long one.

2. Padding a 15-second idea to 30 seconds. Repeating the hook, restating the demo, or adding filler ("and another great thing is…") tells the viewer the video is over before it ends. The long version needs new beats — pain and proof — not longer sentences.

3. Cutting the offer to fit. When a script runs long, creators cut the price and the call to action first. Cut adjectives instead: the demo and the offer are the two beats a sales script cannot lose. Then read the script aloud once, timed, before filming.

FAQ

How many words fit in a 15-second TikTok sales script?

About 35–45 spoken words at a natural pace of roughly 150 words per minute. That is enough for a hook, one pain or demo line, and the offer — nothing more. If your draft runs past 45 words, cut adjectives before you cut the demo or the offer.

How many words fit in a 30-second TikTok sales script?

About 75–90 spoken words at roughly 150 words per minute. That buys the full pitch: hook, pain point, on-camera demo, one proof line and the offer. A 30-second script is not a 15-second script with padding — it is a different structure with room for the pain and proof beats.

Should I make a 15-second or a 30-second TikTok Shop video?

Use 15 seconds when the product is simple, cheap and self-explanatory — the viewer can judge it from one demo shot. Use 30 seconds when the buyer needs a reason first: a problem they have not named yet, a feature that needs showing, or a doubt (noise, size, refills) that one proof line can settle. If you cannot name what the extra 15 seconds are for, make the 15-second version.

Can I just read a 30-second script faster to fit 15 seconds?

No. Speaking twice as fast sounds rushed, viewers stop listening, and the demo — the part that actually persuades — gets squeezed out. Write a separate, shorter script for 15 seconds: one hook, one demo line, one offer.
