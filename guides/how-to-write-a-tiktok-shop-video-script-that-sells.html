How to Write a TikTok Shop Video Script That Sells

A TikTok Shop video that sells follows a repeatable four-beat structure: a hook in the first 3 seconds, the buyer's pain point, a real on-camera demo, then the offer with a clear call to action. Write it to a word count that fits the video length, use only claims your listing can back up, and check the wording before you film.

The numbers to write to

Spoken English runs at roughly 150 words per minute. That gives you a hard budget for each video length:

15 secondsabout 35–45 spoken words — a fast pitch: hook, one pain line, one demo line, offer.
30 secondsabout 75–90 spoken words — the full pitch: hook, pain, demo, proof line, offer.

Source: speaking-pace rule used by SellerScript's script generator (about 150 words per minute); word counts are the generator's own guidance, checked November 2026. If your script runs long, cut adjectives first — never cut the demo or the offer.

The 4-beat structure

1. Hook (first 3 seconds). Say the result or the problem out loud, on camera, before anything else. "I stopped buying desk lamps until I found this one" beats "Hey guys, welcome back" — the viewer decides in the first swipe whether the next 12 seconds are worth it.

2. Pain (one line). Name the exact frustration your buyer already has, in their words. If you sell a mini humidifier, the pain is a dry room and a bulky device that never fits the desk — not a generic claim about comfort.

3. Demo (the longest beat). Show the product doing the thing, in one continuous shot if you can. Point the camera at the feature you named in the pain line, and say the specific fact: size, material, battery life, what is in the box. A demo the viewer can verify is worth more than any adjective.

4. Offer and call to action. State the real price and the real offer, then point at the product link. "It is $12.99 right now — tap the link below" works because every word in it is checkable against the listing.

A worked example: 30-second script

Brief: a foldable mini humidifier, $12.99, audience "students in small dorm rooms". 82 words, about 33 seconds of speaking time:

Hook · 0–7sMy dorm room used to feel dry every single morning — until this little thing showed up.
Pain · 7–12sEvery humidifier I tried was too big for the desk or too loud to sleep next to.
Demo · 12–24sWatch: it folds flat, fits in a drawer, and runs quiet on a USB cable. One fill lasts the whole night.
Offer + CTA · 24–33sIt is $12.99 right now. Tap the link below and check it against your desk space first.

Notice what the script does not do: no "best", no "miracle", no "limited time", no promise the product cannot keep. Every claim is a fact about the product that the listing can confirm. Match the script to the listing

TikTok Shop's Content Policy treats video content as misleading when its claims do not match the product detail page or the packaging. So write the script and the listing from the same facts: the same price, the same features, the same offer. Two practical steps:

Title first, script second. Build the listing title with the title optimizer — core keyword, attributes, scenario, audience — then use the same keyword in the first line of the script. A viewer who searches for the product should hear the words they searched for.

Hashtags last. Once the script is set, take a tiered set from the hashtag generator — broad tags for reach, category tags for the shelf, niche tags for buyers. The caption supports the video; it does not rescue a weak script.

Words to leave out — and why

TikTok Shop's Seller Center guidance (Content Policy and Creator and Content Policy Guide) prohibits misleading claims in product content. The patterns below are the ones that most often get videos flagged or listings pulled. SellerScript's banned-words checker scans for these patterns; its list is SellerScript's own guidance based on public marketplace advertising policy patterns, not TikTok's official word list, so always verify against the current TikTok Shop policy centre before publishing.

Medical claimsCure, heal, treat, lose X lbs, detox, erase wrinkles. Ordinary products cannot claim to cure or treat a condition — use appearance or routine language instead ("skin looks smoother").
Unverifiable superlativesBest, cheapest, #1, perfect, flawless. Unless you can attach proof, use a concrete differentiator: size, material, speed, bundle.
Fake urgencyOnly today, last chance, selling out fast, only 3 left — unless the offer really ends or the stock count is real. Scarcity claims must match real inventory.
Unverifiable proof & false authorityClinically proven, doctor recommended, FDA approved, "official" — only usable with a real study, endorsement or registration you can produce on request.
Income promises & clickbaitGet rich, make money fast, "you won't believe", secret hack. Income promises are banned in marketplace ads; clickbait lowers trust and can limit reach.

One rule covers all five groups: if you cannot prove it from the product, the packaging or the listing, do not say it on camera. A demo does the persuading; the words only have to stay true.

Before you film: a 3-minute checklist

1. Count the words — 35–45 for 15 seconds, 75–90 for 30 seconds. Read it aloud once, timed.

2. Paste the script into the banned-words checker and rewrite every flagged phrase with the safer alternative it suggests.

3. Read the price and offer in the script against the live listing. If they differ by a cent, fix the script, not the listing.

4. Film the demo in one take where possible — a cut between "before" and "after" draws extra scrutiny for beauty, weight and health products.

FAQ

How long should a TikTok Shop video script be?

At about 150 spoken words per minute, 15 seconds needs roughly 35–45 words and 30 seconds about 75–90. Count the words before filming; if the script runs long, cut adjectives before you cut the demo or the offer.

What structure do TikTok Shop sales videos follow?

Hook in the first 3 seconds, the buyer's pain, a real demo, then the offer with a clear call to action. That is the structure SellerScript's script generator builds on.

Which words should I avoid in a TikTok Shop video script?

Avoid medical claims (cure, heal, treat), unverifiable superlatives (best, cheapest, #1), fake urgency (only today, selling out fast when it is not), income promises, and proof claims you cannot back up (clinically proven, FDA approved). Run the script through the banned-words checker before filming.

Should the script match the product listing?

Yes. TikTok Shop treats content as misleading when video claims do not match the product detail page or packaging. Write the script from the same facts as the listing — same price, same features, same offer.
